Original worked practice examples

    
These short examples were written for this website. They are not copied from, or claimed to be, solutions from the uploaded book.

    
1. Constant acceleration
Question: A cyclist starts from rest and accelerates at 2 m/s² for 5 s. Find the final speed and displacement.
Solution: Given u = 0, a = 2 m/s², t = 5 s. Final speed: v = u + at = 0 + 2(5) = 10 m/s. Displacement: s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½(2)(25) = 25 m.

    
2. Newton’s second law
Question: What net force accelerates a 4 kg cart at 3 m/s²?
Solution: F = ma = (4 kg)(3 m/s²) = 12 N.

    
3. Work and kinetic energy
Question: A constant 20 N force moves a box 3 m in the direction of the force. Find the work done.
Solution: W = Fd cos θ. Here θ = 0°, so W = (20)(3)(1) = 60 J.

    
4. Gravitational potential energy
Question: A 2 kg object is lifted 5 m. Use g = 9.8 m/s². Find the increase in gravitational potential energy.
Solution: ΔU = mgh = (2)(9.8)(5) = 98 J.

    
5. Ohm’s law
Question: A 6 Ω resistor is connected across a 12 V source. Find the current.
Solution: I = V/R = 12/6 = 2 A.

    
6. Wave speed
Question: A wave has frequency 8 Hz and wavelength 1.5 m. Find its speed.
Solution: v = fλ = (8 Hz)(1.5 m) = 12 m/s.

EXTRA ENGLISH REVISION

    
✍️ English Quick Practice — 8 Questions

    
Try these extra questions after browsing the English question bank. Choose your answer mentally, then open “Check answer” to see the correct response and a short explanation.

  

  

    
1. She ___ to school every day.
A) go   B) goes   C) going   D) gone
Answer: B) goes. With “she” in the simple present, the verb usually takes -s or -es.

    
2. Choose the closest meaning of “rapid.”
A) slow   B) quick   C) quiet   D) weak
Answer: B) quick. “Rapid” means happening or moving fast.

    
3. Neither of the boys ___ present yesterday.
A) were   B) are   C) was   D) be
Answer: C) was. In formal standard English, “neither” is treated as singular here, and “yesterday” calls for the past tense.

    
4. If I ___ known, I would have helped.
A) have   B) had   C) would   D) has
Answer: B) had. This is a third conditional: “If + past perfect, would have + past participle.”

    
5. Choose the correct passive sentence: “They built the bridge.”
A) The bridge built them.   B) The bridge was built by them.   C) The bridge is build.   D) They were built the bridge.
Answer: B) The bridge was built by them. The simple past passive uses “was/were + past participle.”

    
6. ___ being tired, she continued studying.
A) Despite   B) Although of   C) Because   D) Unless
Answer: A) Despite. “Despite” is followed by a noun or an -ing form; “despite being tired” is correct.

    
7. What does the idiom “break the ice” mean?
A) damage something   B) begin a friendly conversation   C) stop working   D) become angry
Answer: B) begin a friendly conversation. The idiom describes easing tension or helping people start talking.

    
8. Choose the sentence with correct word order.
A) Carefully the completed students assignment their.   B) The students completed their assignment carefully.   C) Their carefully assignment students completed.   D) Completed carefully their students the assignment.
Answer: B) The students completed their assignment carefully. This follows a natural subject–verb–object order, with the adverb at the end.

  

  
These eight questions are additional practice written for this website; they are not represented as official past-paper questions.

Grade 9–12 revision roadmap based on the PDF you uploaded. This is a topic guide with quick-recall prompts, not the original full 2019 entrance-exam paper. Answers and worked solutions have not been added because the source guide does not provide them.

1. Set Theory & Number System
Review: union, intersection, complement, difference, Venn diagrams, rational and irrational numbers.
Quick recall: If A = {1,2,3} and B = {2,3,4}, find A ∪ B, A ∩ B and A − B. Is √5 rational or irrational?

    
2. Solving Equations & Inequalities
Review: linear equations, systems by substitution/elimination, quadratic and radical equations, absolute-value and quadratic inequalities.
Quick recall: Solve 2x − 3 = 7 and |x − 2| ≤ 5. Solve x + y = 5, 2x − y = 1.

    
3. Relations, Functions & Special Functions
Review: domain/range, inverse functions, polynomials, rational functions and asymptotes, exponents, logarithms, trigonometry, modulus, signum and floor functions.
Quick recall: Find f⁻¹(x) for f(x) = 2x + 1. Find the zeros of x³ − 6x² + 11x − 6. Evaluate ⌊−2.1⌋ and sin 30°.

    
4. Congruency, Similarity, Geometry & Coordinate Geometry
Review: similar figures, polygon angles, circle theorems, arc length, sector area, cylinder/cone/sphere formulas, distance and midpoint, parallel and perpendicular lines.
Quick recall: Similar triangles have corresponding sides 3 and 6; find the ratio of their areas. Find the distance between (1,2) and (4,6).

    
5. Vectors & Transformations
Review: scalar and vector quantities, dot product, translation, reflection and rotation.
Quick recall: Find the dot product of a = (3,4) and b = (1,2). Reflect (2,−3) over the x-axis and y-axis.

    
6. Matrices & Determinants
Review: matrix operations, 2×2 inverse, determinants, Gaussian elimination and Cramer's rule.
Quick recall: Find the inverse of A = [[2,1],[5,3]]. Solve x + y = 3 and 2x − y = 0 using Cramer's rule.

    
7. Statistics & Probability
Review: data types and graphs, mean/median/mode, range, variance, standard deviation, sampling, counting, permutations, combinations and probability.
Quick recall: Find the mean, median and mode of 4, 7, 7, 9, 10, 12. For one die roll, find P(even) and P(greater than 4).

    
8. Sequences & Series
Review: arithmetic and geometric sequences, finite/infinite sums and sigma notation.
Quick recall: Find the 10th term and the sum of the first 10 terms of 3, 7, 11, 15, …

    
9. Limits, Continuity, Derivatives & Integration
Review: limits, continuity, power and chain rules, critical points, integration techniques, definite integrals and area between curves.
Quick recall: Differentiate f(x) = 3x⁴ − 2x² + 5. Evaluate ∫(3x² + 2x) dx and ∫₀¹ x² dx.

    
10. Linear Programming
Review: graphing constraints, feasible regions, corner points and optimization of an objective function.
Quick recall: Maximize Z = 3x + 2y subject to x + y ≤ 4, x ≥ 0 and y ≥ 0.

    
11. Mathematical Applications in Business
Review: simple and compound interest, present/future value, savings, borrowing, loan repayment and taxation/VAT.
Quick recall: Find the compound amount on 10,000 birr at 8% per year for 3 years, compounded annually.

Lesson 2: Antonyms and Synonyms
Study: Similar and opposite meanings, context-based vocabulary and common SAT word lists.
Quick recall: Name three strategies for using context clues to understand an unknown word.

Study: Skimming, scanning, main idea, inference, detail questions, author’s purpose and tone, and vocabulary in context.
Quick recall: Explain the difference between skimming and scanning.

      

      

        
Lesson 4: Analytical and Logical Reasoning
Study: Logical deduction, syllogisms, assumptions, conclusions, and arrangement, ordering and matching puzzles.
Quick recall: Form a valid conclusion from “All A are B” and “All B are C.”

      

      

        
Lesson 5: Grammar and Sentence Correction
Study: Parts of speech, subject–verb agreement, tense, sentence structure, run-ons, fragments, parallelism, who/whom and affect/effect.
Quick recall: Identify the error in “Each of the students have submitted their work.”

      

      

        
Lesson 6: Vocabulary in Context
Study: Definition, synonym, antonym and inference clues; high-frequency words; roots, prefixes and suffixes.
Quick recall: What does the root “bene-” mean? Give two examples.

      

      

        
Lesson 7: Number System and Arithmetic
Study: Number types, order of operations, fractions, decimals, percentages, divisibility rules, LCM and HCF.
Quick recall: Find the LCM and HCF of 12 and 18.

      

      

        
Lesson 8: Ratios, Percentages, and Averages
Study: Direct and inverse proportion, percentage change, discounts, tax, mean, median, mode, speed, distance, time and work rates.
Quick recall: A price increases from $80 to $100. What is the percentage increase?

      

      

        
Lesson 9: Probability and Statistics
Study: Single and multiple events, conditional probability, permutations, combinations, measures of central tendency and spread, tables and graphs.
Quick recall: In how many ways can 3 books be chosen from 5?

      

      

        
Lesson 10: Sequence and Series
Study: Arithmetic and geometric sequences and series, number/letter/symbol patterns, and sums.
Quick recall: Find the 10th term of 3, 7, 11, 15, …

      

      

        
Lesson 11: Time, Age, and Business Applications
Study: Clock and calendar problems, age equations and ratios, simple/compound interest, profit, loss, discount, commission and partnership.
Quick recall: What is the compound interest on $1,000 at 10% per year for 2 years?

      

      

        
Lesson 12: Equations, Inequalities, and Functions
Study: Linear equations and systems, quadratic equations, linear/compound inequalities, domain, range, graphs, exponential and logarithmic functions.
Quick recall: Solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0.

      

      

        
Lesson 13: Geometry, Trigonometry, and Calendar
Study: Lines, angles, triangles, quadrilaterals, circle measurements, 3D shapes, Pythagorean theorem, coordinate geometry, basic trigonometry and calendar calculations.
Quick recall: Find the distance between (1, 2) and (4, 6).

      

      

        
Lesson 14: Data Interpretation
Study: Reading tables, bar graphs, pie charts and line graphs; calculating from visual data; comparing data sets and drawing conclusions.
Quick recall: A pie chart shows 25% for Science. What angle does that represent?

      

      

        
Lesson 15: Quantitative Comparisons
Study: Compare Column A with Column B; decide larger, smaller or equal; use data sufficiency, simplification, substitution and approximation.
Quick recall: Column A: 3² + 4². Column B: 5². Which is greater?

ABOUT THE PROJECT

    
About Student Toolkit

    
A free, student-focused website for studying, planning, and entrance-exam preparation.

  

  

    

      
🎯 What does this website do?

      
Student Toolkit brings useful study tools and learning materials together in one place. Students can plan study sessions, manage tasks, take notes, create flashcards, use a focus timer, calculate grades, and revise for exams.

      
It also includes Ethiopian University Entrance Exam (EUEE) revision sections for Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, and English, plus SAT/Aptitude topics and practice questions.

    

    

      
📚 Who is it for?

      
It is designed for students who want a simple way to organize their learning, especially students preparing for exams. The website is designed to work on phones, tablets, and computers.

      
Study workflow: Plan → Study → Track → Review.

    

    

      
💡 Why was it created?

      
The goal is to make helpful study tools and revision resources easier to access in one place, without requiring a paid subscription for the core tools.

    

    

      
📌 Using the study materials

      
Some question collections are based on supplied study documents. Not every collection includes an official or verified answer key; check uncertain answers with a teacher or trusted textbook.
